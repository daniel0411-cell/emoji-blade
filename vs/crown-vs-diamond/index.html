Attack and speed versus mass and endurance

Crown has higher attack (88 vs 72) and speed (86 vs 65), but lower mass (86 vs 96) and endurance (88 vs 98). Diamond loses less passive energy. Crown’s higher attack does not protect it from incoming damage; at the same relative speed and opponent attack, its lower own mass increases the received-damage factor. Neither preset has a shield or a special royal skill.

Formula examples at 70% power

Passive energy loss is 0.048 + (100 - endurance) × 0.00055 per physics step. Over 100 steps, Crown loses 5.460 and Diamond 4.910: a difference of 0.550. At 70% power, speed / 100 × 14 × power / 100 gives initial speeds of 8.428 and 6.370 internal units/step. Crown starts about 32% faster. These isolated calculations exclude collisions, Dash and rail costs; steps are not seconds, RPM or a battle-duration prediction. They do not establish a winner.

What these comparisons can tell you

Ratings describe trade-offs, not measured win rates. There are no elemental counters or shape-specific damage. Standard walls rebound tops. In Hazard mode, a top can lose by exiting a side pocket at sufficient speed, during Dash, or during Xtreme acceleration; slow pocket contacts rebound. On a closing collision, Dash, a 28% random check, or relative speed above 7.5 simulation units can trigger an ordinary critical for 1.5x damage to both tops. An active Xtreme rail boost makes it 1.8x. These multipliers affect damage, not collision impulse.

Try a repeatable local test

The button selects both tops in local 2-player mode without starting a battle. Use the standard arena. Player 1 sets 70% power and -35°, then Player 2 sets 70% and -145°. These inward mirrored angles are a starting point, not an optimized build.

  1. Keep both presets and the arena fixed, and do not Dash in the first comparison. Repeat rounds; random critical hits can change results.
  2. Swap which top starts on the left and mirror its angle for the new side. Record both positions rather than treating one side as a character advantage.
  3. Restore the starting setup, then change only Player 1 power to 85%. Keep Player 2 at 70% and both angles unchanged. Compare routes and energy; test other angles in a separate series.

Record the date and game version, both tops and sides, arena, both power/angle settings, Dash use, rail contacts, critical hits, remaining energy and outcome. Keep Hazard tests separate. These instructions are a test plan, not a published sample or win-rate claim; local play does not advance stages or streaks.
