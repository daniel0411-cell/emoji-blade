Hazard Arena, Ring-Outs & Xtreme Rails

Updated November 13, 2026

Learn when Hazard arena side pockets cause ring-outs, how Xtreme rails and Dash affect risk, and how to compare standard and Hazard local duels.

Standard walls and Hazard pockets

Standard arenas rebound tops at the boundary. Hazard mode opens pockets on the left and right sides. A ring-out can end the round even while the top has energy remaining. The upper and lower arcs are Xtreme rails, not those exit pockets.

Read the boundary before planning a route

A rail contact and a pocket exit are different events. A boosted top can leave a rail and later reach a side pocket; the rail itself is not an exit. Compare the mirrored launch directions, then observe where your actual first boundary contact occurs.

Keep the canvas dimensions and arena radius fixed when comparing standard and Hazard trials.

When an exit becomes a loss

At a side pocket, a top can continue outward when its movement is fast enough or when Dash or Xtreme acceleration is active. It must travel beyond the exit threshold before a ring-out is recorded. Slow pocket contacts without a boost rebound. Merely touching the boundary is not sufficient, and mass does not confer immunity.

What an Xtreme rail costs

A spinning top can receive a boost at an upper or lower rail when its rail cooldown is ready and it has more than 8 energy. The boost spends 3 energy and redirects movement inward with a tangential component. A closing collision during the active boost deals 1.8 times damage to both tops. More speed can also make a subsequent side-pocket exit more likely.

Dash is a choice with a cost

Player 1 can Dash with Space or the battle button; local Player 2 uses Enter or their own button. A Dash requires more than 6 energy, spends 6 and has a one-second simulation cooldown. It boosts movement in the current direction, so using it while heading toward a pocket can put your own top at risk. A Dash clash without Xtreme uses the ordinary 1.5 times critical multiplier.

Compare arena modes fairly

  1. Open the same pair in local two-player mode and record both launch settings.
  2. Play several standard rounds without Dash, recording rail contacts and collisions.
  3. Repeat in Hazard mode with the same settings. Record ring-outs separately from energy depletion.
  4. Swap sides, then change one launch angle at a time. Add Dash only as a separate trial.

No fixed angle guarantees a ring-out win. Collisions, boost timing and the rival's path can redirect either top.

Both tops can fall out

If both recorded ring-outs occur within 60 milliseconds, the game declares a draw. Otherwise the later exit wins the double ring-out comparison. Judge the result screen rather than assuming the first visible fall settles the round. Local tests preserve solo progression; a pocket win there does not advance your stage.
