Dash Timing & Critical Clash Guide

2026-11-12

Understand Dash controls, energy cost, cooldown and critical damage, then compare recorded attempts without assuming a guaranteed win.

What does Dash actually do?

Dash accelerates the top along its current movement direction. It does not aim toward the opponent, steer, teleport or restore energy. It adds up to 6 speed units toward a target speed of 18; it does not slow a top already moving faster than 18. Movement still loses speed through damping and collisions afterward.

When can I use it?

Dash works only during an active battle, after launch, while the top is spinning and moving. It requires more than 6 energy and no remaining Dash cooldown. A successful activation immediately spends 6 energy, even if you miss the opponent. Player 1 uses the Dash button or Space; local Player 2 uses their button or Enter. Keyboard shortcuts are ignored while an input, slider, button or link has focus.

Why does a critical hurt my own top?

In a closing top-to-top collision, an active Dash makes the collision critical. Ordinary criticals multiply damage to BOTH tops by 1.5; an active Xtreme boost makes the multiplier 1.8. A 28% random check or relative speed above 7.5 can also produce an ordinary critical without Dash. The multiplier does not multiply collision impulse. A critical is not armor, immunity or a guaranteed favorable trade.

A cooldown is not an instruction to Dash

The cooldown lasts 60 simulation steps, about one second at 60 Hz. The Dash effect lasts up to 12 steps and is consumed by a qualifying smash collision; it does not stay active throughout the cooldown. There is no single best timing for every moving opponent. Watch the current travel direction and distance before trying a Dash. Spending the last available energy on a missed acceleration can shorten survival.

Extra caution in Hazard mode

In Hazard mode, an active Dash can qualify a top for a ring-out at a side pocket even when its speed is below the normal speed threshold. A Dash toward a side opening is therefore a risk to your own top. Standard walls rebound instead. Xtreme rail boosts are a separate mechanic with a separate energy cost; their presence changes a comparison, so record the arena and any rail contact.

Compare a Dash attempt with a baseline

Start a Fire-versus-Fire local duel in the standard arena. Set both powers to 70%, Player 1 angle to -35 degrees and Player 2 to -145 degrees as a trial setup, not an optimum. Play five rounds without Dash, then five with one Player 1 Dash when the tops approach each other. Keep Player 2 Dash unused and record the activation moment, hits, result and remaining energy. Criticals remain random, so ten rounds are exploratory rather than a validated win-rate claim. Repeat with a different timing before deciding whether the energy trade is useful.
