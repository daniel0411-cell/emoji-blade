Launch Power & Angle Guide

Updated November 13, 2026

Learn what launch power and angle change in Emoji Blade, use mirrored local duel settings and compare repeatable trials without claiming a best build.

What power actually changes

Launch power ranges from 30% to 100%. Initial movement speed is proportional to the selected power and the top's speed rating. At the instant of launch, changing the same top from 70% to 100% makes it move about 1.43 times as fast. It does not give more starting energy: both launches begin at 100 energy. Bowl pull, damping, rail boosts and collisions change movement after launch.

Read angles from each starting side

The left top starts to the left of the arena center and the right top starts to the right. Angles use screen coordinates: 0 degrees points right, -90 points up and 90 points down. For an inward, upward launch, Player 1 can try -35 degrees and Player 2 -145 degrees. Copying -35 degrees to both players does not create a symmetric launch.

Two sides need mirrored directions

After launch, bowl pull, collisions, walls and rails redirect movement. Use the Hazard arena map to distinguish an exit pocket from a boosting rail.

A small experiment you can repeat

  1. Choose the same preset for both players in local two-player mode. Keep the standard arena selected.
  2. Start both players at 70% power, with Player 1 at -35 degrees and Player 2 at -145 degrees. These are trial settings, not a proven optimum.
  3. Record the settings and result for several rounds. Leave Dash unused in this first comparison.
  4. Change only Player 1 power to 85%, then repeat. Next, restore power and compare another angle.
  5. Record rail contacts and critical clashes as well as the winner. Random criticals mean identical inputs need not yield identical outcomes.

Why full power can lose

More speed can produce a harder clash, but collision damage affects both tops. A launch that reaches a rail may receive another speed boost; in Hazard mode, it may instead escape through a side pocket. Compare first-contact paths and remaining energy rather than treating maximum power as a damage upgrade.

Keep a useful battle log

Use columns for both tops, arena mode, each player's power and angle, Dash use, rail contacts and outcome. Keep standard and Hazard results separate. Swap sides before attributing a result to a preset. A few rounds are practice observations, not a measured universal win rate.

Record the arena size as well as the settings

Arena radius depends on the actual canvas container width and height; starting positions sit 0.52 arena radii to either side of center. Top radius does not scale proportionally with the arena. The same power and angles on different screens therefore do not reproduce the same geometry.

For a useful battle log, record the game version, browser, viewport, actual CSS canvas width and height, arena radius, both presets and sides, power and angles, arena mode, Dash use, critical clashes, rail contacts and outcome. Keep the window size unchanged within a comparison. If you cannot record these dimensions, label the observations as practice rather than a controlled comparison.

Solo practice has an extra variable

In solo mode, the rival's launch angle varies and its power depends on progression; Bosses launch at full power. Local two-player trials let you set both launches. Local practice does not advance stages or streaks and cannot recreate progression Boss bonuses merely by selecting the same emoji.
