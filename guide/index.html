Emoji Blade transforms Unicode characters into spinning tops with dimensional artwork and simplified 2D physics. The objective is simple: outspin your opponent by depleting their energy.

1. The Slingshot Launch System

You have two intuitive methods to control your launch:

  • Arena Touch/Mouse Drag: Press and hold directly on your spinning top inside the circular stadium. Drag backward to aim the glowing trajectory line, and release to shoot!
  • Control Panel Sliders: Fine-tune your Launch Power (30% - 100%) and Launch Angle (-180° to +180°), then press Launch. Both methods start the same countdown before the tops move.

Start with 85% power and -35 degrees in solo mode. After a round, change only power or angle so you can compare the effect. The rival's launch and critical clashes include randomness, so repeat the setup before drawing conclusions.

Dash during a battle

Once a battle starts, Player 1 can use the Dash button or Space. In local two-player mode, Player 2 can use their Dash button or Enter. A dash spends 6 energy and has a one-second simulation cooldown; it needs more than 6 energy remaining. It is unavailable during setup, the countdown or after the battle ends.

2. Understanding Top Physics & Geometry

Your spinning top is governed by four core metrics:

  • Mass (Weight): Determines momentum resistance. Heavier tops suffer less recoil when hit, reducing energy loss from collisions.
  • Attack: Contributes to energy damage dealt on direct collision.
  • Speed (Rating): Affects rotational frequency. Speed also affects launch velocity. Critical hits also depend on Dash, Xtreme boosts, relative collision speed and a random chance.
  • Endurance (Stamina): Governs how slowly your top loses energy over time.

3. Critical Clashes: 1.5x and Xtreme 1.8x

A closing collision is critical during Dash, with a 28% random chance, or above 7.5 relative speed in simulation units. Ordinary critical damage is 1.5x for both tops; an active Xtreme rail boost raises it to 1.8x. These multipliers affect damage, not collision impulse.

4. Stage Bosses & 2P Local Mode

Every 5th stage in Streak Mode features a massive Stage Boss (such as 👹 Oni Demon or 👾 Retro Invader) with 35% extra mass and 25% extra attack. Decorative armor teeth do not change collision geometry. Manage your launch angle and power to deplete their spin reserve.

Mass versus speed: change one variable

Mass affects collision response and damage received; speed affects launch movement and visual rotation. Compare Diamond (96 mass, 65 speed) and Lightning (60 mass, 99 speed) under recorded launch settings. They also differ in attack and endurance, so this comparison cannot isolate mass alone or establish a universal best ratio.

Endurance and passive energy drain

Passive drain per 60 Hz simulation step is 0.048 + (100 − endurance) × 0.00055. Moai at 100 endurance loses 0.048 energy per step; Fire at 68 loses 0.0656. This excludes collision losses, so it does not predict total survival time. More endurance reduces passive drain, not incoming damage.

Boss and streak checklist

The first stage 5 Oni has mass 149 and attack 125; the first stage 10 Invader has mass 97 and attack 108. Only solo progression wins advance the stage and streak. A draw preserves both; a loss resets the current streak but stays on the stage. Local and custom practice do not replace the boosted encounters.

Arena modes and Xtreme rails

Standard arena walls rebound the tops. In Hazard mode, the left and right pockets can cause a ring-out loss when a top exits at sufficient speed or during Dash or an Xtreme boost; slow pocket contacts rebound.

The upper and lower rails can grant a short Xtreme speed boost when a spinning top has more than 8 energy and its rail cooldown is ready. The boost spends 3 energy and makes closing collisions deal 1.8x damage to both tops. In Hazard mode, the extra speed also increases the risk of exiting a side pocket.
